Round Robin Bet Types and Combinations

Round robin betting structures vary by parlay size, determining how many selections combine in each wager. The most common configurations are two-team parlays (2-leg), three-team parlays (3-leg), and four-team parlays (4-leg), each creating different numbers of individual bets from the same pool of selections. Four selections arranged as 2-leg round robins generate six separate parlays, whilst the same four selections as 3-leg produce four parlays. With five selections, bettors create ten 2-leg parlays or ten 3-leg parlays, demonstrating how combination mathematics scale rapidly.

Two-Team Parlay Combinations

Two-team parlays represent the most popular round robin format, pairing every possible combination of two selections from the total pool. The mathematical formula C(n,2) calculates total combinations, where n equals the number of selections. Five picks create ten distinct two-team parlays, whilst six picks generate fifteen parlays. This structure offers lower payouts per individual parlay compared to larger combinations, but provides more opportunities to secure winning returns. Two-team configurations typically appeal to bettors prioritising volume of chances over individual parlay value, spreading risk across multiple smaller wagers rather than fewer high-risk combinations.

Three-Team Parlay Combinations

Three-team parlays increase potential returns per wager whilst reducing the total number of parlays created from identical selections. The formula C(n,3) determines combinations—five selections produce ten three-team parlays, matching the count of 2-leg combinations but requiring three correct picks per parlay instead of two. Six selections generate twenty 3-leg parlays, substantially more than the fifteen 2-leg combinations from the same pool. This format balances risk and reward between conservative two-team structures and aggressive four-team or larger parlays, appealing to bettors seeking moderate upside without extreme exposure.

Four-Team Round Robin Example

Consider four selections with odds of 2.0, 1.8, 2.2, and 1.9 respectively. A two-team round robin with £5 stake per parlay creates six separate wagers, requiring £30 total investment. If all four selections win, every parlay succeeds, returning approximately £108 for £78 profit. This graduated outcome structure demonstrates round robin betting's core advantage—partial success generates partial returns rather than total loss.

Five-Team Round Robin Example

Five selections create substantially more combinations than four. A 2-leg round robin generates ten parlays at £5 stake each, totalling £50 investment. Alternatively, structuring the same five selections as a 3-leg round robin also creates ten parlays (costing £50), but requires three correct picks per parlay instead of two. The 3-leg format offers higher per-parlay returns but lower overall success probability, demonstrating the risk-reward trade-off between parlay sizes.

Six-Team Round Robin Cost Analysis

Six selections illustrate exponential combination growth. A 2-leg configuration produces fifteen parlays, 3-leg creates twenty parlays, and 4-leg generates fifteen parlays again.
