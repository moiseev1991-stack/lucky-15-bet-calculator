What Is a Heinz Flag Bet?

A Heinz Flag is a complex wager combining 6 selections into 87 separate bets. The structure includes 15 doubles, 20 trebles, 15 four-folds, 6 five-folds, and 1 six-fold accumulator, plus 15 Up-And-Down Single Stakes About Pairs. This bet type extends the standard 57-bet Heinz by adding the SSA component, creating additional return pathways when selections win in sequence.

Heinz Flag Calculator processes these 87 bets automatically, calculating potential returns based on entered odds and stake amounts. The calculator removes manual computation errors common when working with multi-layered bet structures. Each bet component operates independently, meaning partial wins generate returns even when not all 6 selections succeed.

The composition differs from simpler accumulators by distributing risk across multiple bet tiers. Where a standard accumulator requires all selections to win, the Heinz Flag pays out on any two winning selections through the doubles component. The 15 Up-And-Down SSA Pairs function as conditional bets, where winnings from one selection automatically stake on another in both directions.

Heinz Flag Composition - All 87 Bets Explained

The 87-bet structure breaks down into six distinct bet tiers, each formed through combinatorial mathematics applied to the 6 selections. Understanding how each tier constructs reveals why the Heinz Flag offers broader coverage than standard accumulators while requiring substantial stake commitment.

Doubles and Trebles Formation

The 15 doubles emerge from pairing any two selections from the six available (combinations formula: 6C2 = 15). If selections are labeled A, B, C, D, E, F, then doubles include AB, AC, AD, AE, AF, BC, BD, BE, BF, CD, CE, CF, DE, DF, EF. The 20 trebles follow identical logic using three-selection combinations (6C3 = 20), covering groups like ABC, ABD, ABE through to DEF. Heinz Flag Calculator lists each double and treble separately with corresponding odds multiplication.

Four-Folds, Five-Folds, and Six-Fold

The accumulator tiers comprise 15 four-fold bets (6C4), 6 five-fold bets (6C5), and 1 six-fold bet using all selections. These bets require sequential wins to pay out, with odds multiplying across all included selections. The six-fold represents the maximum potential return but also the lowest probability outcome. This structure mirrors the Lucky 63 accumulator component, though Lucky 63 includes 6 additional single bets while Heinz Flag substitutes Single Stakes About Pairs.

Up-And-Down Single Stakes About Pairs

The 15 SSA Pairs constitute the Heinz Flag's defining feature, separating it from the standard 57-bet Heinz. Each pair operates as two conditional bets: if selection A wins, proceeds stake on selection B, and simultaneously if B wins, proceeds stake on A. This creates 30 individual conditional bets, but they count as 15 pairs in the 87-bet total. Heinz Flag Calculator processes SSA Pairs distinctly from doubles because the stake mechanics differ—doubles pay out immediately when both selections win, while SSA Pairs reinvest winnings sequentially. The Up-And-Down structure means both directions activate simultaneously, unlike Single Stakes About bets where only one direction applies.

Each Way Heinz Flag Bets

An each-way Heinz Flag doubles the total bet count from 87 to 174 bets, as each selection is backed to both win and place. This creates 87 win bets and 87 place bets running in parallel, meaning the total stake is doubled. For example, a £1 unit stake becomes £174 rather than £87, which requires careful budget planning.

Place terms vary by sport and bookmaker but typically follow industry standards. In horse racing, place bets usually pay at one-quarter or one-fifth of the win odds, depending on field size. Races with eight or more runners often apply one-fifth odds for the top three places, while smaller fields may use one-quarter odds for the top two or three. These terms directly affect potential returns, as the place portion of the bet settles at reduced odds even when selections finish outside the winning position.

Each-way betting is particularly common in horse racing events but can also apply to markets like Premier League top scorer bets or tournament winner selections where bookmakers offer place terms. The Heinz Flag Calculator accounts for both win and place components when calculating total returns, making it essential to input accurate place terms and confirm how many places are paid. This bet type suits scenarios where bettors expect some selections to place rather than win outright, spreading risk across both outcomes while maintaining the multiple bet structure.

Dead Heat and Void Bet Handling

When using Heinz Flag Calculator, understanding Dead Heat and Void rules is essential for accurate return projections. A Dead Heat occurs when two or more selections tie for a winning position; the stake is divided by the number of tied participants, and the full odds are applied to the divided stake. For example, in a two-way Dead Heat at 3/1 fractional odds, half the stake returns at full odds rather than the full stake at reduced odds.

Void selections present a different challenge across the 87 bets in a Heinz Flag. When one selection is voided, all accumulators containing that leg are recalculated as if the bet type reduced by one selection. A voided selection in a treble converts it to a double; a voided selection in a double converts it to a single. The calculator automatically adjusts the bet count and potential returns accordingly. With five valid selections remaining, the effective bet becomes a smaller Flag covering only those active legs.
