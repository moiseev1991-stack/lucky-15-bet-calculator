What Is a Union Jack Patent Bet?

A Union Jack Patent is a 9-selection combination bet consisting of 56 total bets: 8 trebles, 24 doubles, and 24 singles. This bet type requires only one winning selection to generate a return, though multiple winners significantly increase profit. The structure distributes stake across all 56 combinations, meaning a £1 per bet stake equals a £56 total outlay in GBP.

The Patent bet format is popular in United Kingdom horse racing and football betting markets. Bettors choose nine different outcomes—typically horses, teams, or matches—and the calculator constructs every possible combination automatically. The 8 trebles cover all three-selection groups from the nine picks. The 24 doubles include every two-selection pairing. The 24 singles back each selection individually.

Union Jack Patent Calculator processes these combinations instantly, showing potential returns for scenarios ranging from one winner through all nine winning. This visibility helps bettors assess risk before placing stakes. The bet type suits situations where a bettor has multiple strong fancies but wants coverage if only some succeed.

Each component of the 56 bets settles independently. If three selections win, the bettor collects on one treble (all three winners), three doubles (each pair of winners), and three singles (each individual winner). Seven of the 56 bets produce returns in this scenario. If five selections win, the calculation expands: multiple trebles, more doubles, and five singles all generate payouts.

The structure differs from simpler combination bets. A Trixie uses three selections to create four bets (three doubles and one treble). A Yankee uses four selections for eleven bets. The Union Jack Patent's nine selections and 56 bets provide broader coverage but demand higher total stakes. Bettors in the United Kingdom often deploy this bet type during major racing festivals when confident across multiple races but uncertain which specific horses will win.

Each-Way Union Jack Patent Bets

An each-way Union Jack Patent doubles the total number of bets from 56 to 112 by placing both win and place components on every combination. The standard Patent bet comprises 24 singles, 24 doubles, and 8 trebles across 9 selections, totalling 56 bets. Adding each-way terms creates a mirror structure of 56 place bets alongside the 56 win bets. At £1 per bet, the total stake rises from £56 to £112.

Place terms determine the returns on the place portion. In horse racing, bookmakers typically offer one-quarter of the win odds for races with five to seven runners, or one-fifth of the win odds for eight or more runners. The place portion covers the top two, three, or four positions depending on field size and race type. When a selection places but does not win, the place bet still returns at the reduced odds, providing partial compensation even when the win portion loses.

Each-way betting suits scenarios where bettors expect strong performances without outright victories. A horse finishing second at 10/1 odds with quarter-place terms would return at 2.5/1 on the place portion. This structure reduces risk across the 112 bets, though the doubled stake requires careful bankroll management.

Each-way Union Jack Patents are common in horse racing, particularly on competitive handicaps or festival meetings where multiple fancies compete. The format is less prevalent in football betting, where markets typically lack place terms. Bettors should verify place conditions before staking, as terms vary by bookmaker, event type, and number of participants. How Returns Are Calculated: Odds Combination Formula

Understanding how returns accumulate across a patent bet requires knowledge of odds multiplication. Each combination bet generates its own return, calculated independently before being summed into the total payout.

Doubles Calculation Method

When two selections combine in a double, their decimal odds multiply together. If selection A wins at 3.0 and selection B wins at 4.0, the combined odds become 3.0 × 4.0 = 12.0. A £1 stake on this double returns £12, comprising the £1 stake plus £11 profit. With 24 doubles in the patent structure, each pairing of the nine selections creates a separate calculation when both legs win.

Trebles Calculation Method

Trebles follow the same multiplication principle across three selections. If selections A (3.0), B (4.0), and C (2.5) all win, the combined odds equal 3.0 × 4.0 × 2.5 = 30.0. A £1 stake returns £30. The patent contains eight trebles, requiring three correct outcomes for each to generate a return.

Total Return Aggregation

Total payout equals the sum of returns from all winning bets. If four selections win from nine, all singles on those four pay out, plus all doubles where both legs succeeded, plus any trebles where all three legs won. The 56 individual bets settle independently based on their specific selection combinations.

Rule 4 Deductions

When non-runners are declared after bet placement, Rule 4 deductions reduce odds proportionally on remaining selections according to the withdrawn runner's price. These adjustments apply before odds multiplication occurs in combination bets.
