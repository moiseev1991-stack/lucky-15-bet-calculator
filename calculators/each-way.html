What Is an Each Way Bet and How Does It Work?

An each way bet consists of two separate wagers: a win bet and a place bet. When you place £5 each way, you're actually staking £10 total—£5 on your selection to win and £5 on it to place within a set number of positions determined by the race conditions. The each way calculator works out returns for both parts of this bet using your stake, odds, and the place terms set by the bookmaker, giving you a clear picture of potential winnings before you commit your stake.

Understanding how each way betting works is essential before using the calculator. The win portion pays out only if your selection finishes first, while the place portion pays if it finishes within the specified number of places. Both bets use your chosen stake amount, which is why the total cost doubles.

Place Terms and Fractions Explained

Place terms determine how much you win from the place portion of your each way bet. Common place terms are 1/4 and 1/5 of the win odds. If your selection has odds of 8/1 and the place terms are 1/4, the place odds become 2/1 (8 divided by 4). The each way calculator applies these place terms automatically when you enter the win odds and select the fraction. The number of places paid varies by race—typically three places for 8+ runners, two places for 5-7 runners. Understanding place terms is essential for working out your potential returns before placing the bet.

Why Your Total Stake Doubles

Many bettors misunderstand the total cost of an each way bet. A £5 each way bet costs £10 in total because you're placing two separate £5 bets—one on the win and one on the place. This doubling of your stake matters for bankroll management. The each way calculator displays both the individual stakes and total stake clearly, helping you budget your betting funds accurately and avoid unexpected costs when placing each way wagers.

How to Use the Each Way Calculator

The each way calculator requires four key inputs: your stake amount, the odds format (fractional or decimal), the place terms fraction, and the number of places paid. Enter these details to instantly calculate your total returns if your selection wins, places, or both. The calculator shows separate win-only and place-only returns alongside the combined total.

  1. Enter your stake: Input the amount you want to bet each way. Remember this will be doubled for the total cost (£5 each way = £10 total stake).
  2. Select odds format: Choose between fractional (e.g., 8/1) or decimal (e.g., 9.0) depending on how your bookmaker displays odds.
  3. Input the odds: Enter the win odds for your selection as shown by the bookmaker.
  4. Set place terms: Select the place terms fraction, typically 1/4 or 1/5 of the win odds. This information appears in the race details.
  5. Choose number of places: Enter how many positions are paid as places. This varies by race size—usually three places for 8+ runners.
  6. Review the results: The calculator displays your total stake, potential win returns, place-only returns, and combined winnings if your selection wins.

Worked Example: £10 Each Way at 8/1

Consider a £10 each way bet at odds of 8/1 with 1/4 place terms and three places paid. Your total stake is £20 (£10 win + £10 place). If your selection wins, the win bet returns £90 (£10 × 8 = £80 profit + £10 stake back). The place bet pays at 2/1 odds (8/1 ÷ 4 = 2/1), returning £30 (£10 × 2 = £20 profit + £10 stake back). Your total return for a win is £120. If your selection only places (finishes second or third), you lose the win bet but collect £30 from the place bet, giving you a net profit of £10 after deducting the £20 total stake. The each way calculator performs these calculations instantly.

How Each Way Winnings Are Calculated

An each way calculator determines returns using two formulas. The win bet pays stake × odds + stake if the selection wins. The place bet pays stake × place odds + stake for placed finishes. Place odds apply the place terms (1/4 or 1/5) to win odds. Total returns depend on final position.

The win portion follows a straightforward calculation identical to standard win betting. For a £10 stake at 8/1 odds, a winning selection returns £80 in winnings plus the original £10 stake returned, totaling £90. This win bet calculation remains unchanged whether betting win-only or placing an each way bet.

The place portion requires applying the advertised place terms to the original win odds. With 1/4 place terms, 8/1 odds become 2/1 for the place bet calculation (8 ÷ 4 = 2). A £10 place stake at 2/1 returns £20 in winnings plus the £10 stake back, totaling £30. An each way calculator automatically converts win odds to place odds based on the specified terms for the race.

When both bets succeed because the selection wins, the combined return adds both payouts together. The £10 each way bet (£20 total stake) at 8/1 with 1/4 terms returns £90 from the win bet plus £30 from the place bet, totaling £120 overall. If the selection only places without winning outright, only the place bet pays out, returning £30 while the £10 win stake is lost for a net loss.

How Many Places Are Paid?

The number of paid places varies by race size and type. Fields with 5-7 runners typically pay two places at 1/4 odds. Races with 8-15 runners usually extend to three places at 1/5 odds. Handicap races with 16 or more runners often pay four places at 1/4 odds. Some bookmakers offer extra place promotions on major events, adding an additional paid position beyond standard terms. The Grand National and Cheltenham Festival frequently feature enhanced place terms. An each way calculator automatically applies the correct place terms based on race conditions, ensuring accurate return projections across different scenarios.

Which Sports Offer Each Way Betting?

Each way betting is primarily available in horse racing, where it originated and remains most popular. The format also features prominently in golf tournaments, particularly for outright winner markets. Greyhound racing offers each way options on most events. Selected motorsports and other outright markets occasionally provide each way terms, though availability varies significantly by bookmaker and event.

Horse racing accounts for the majority of each way betting volume across British bookmakers. Every race meeting features each way options, with terms varying by field size and race classification. The structure suits racing's competitive fields where multiple horses can realistically place. Other sports have adopted the format for specific market types, particularly long-term outright competitions.

Horse Racing: The Core Each Way Market

Horse racing remains the primary each way betting market across all bookmakers. Standard place terms apply automatically: 1/4 odds for two or three places, 1/5 odds for larger fields. Non-handicap races typically pay fewer places than handicap events with similar field sizes. Major festivals like the Cheltenham Festival and Grand National attract significant each way betting activity, often with enhanced place terms offered as promotions. Daily race cards at British and Irish tracks consistently feature each way options, making it the default format for many racing bettors.

Golf Tournaments and Each Way Odds

Golf tournaments represent the second major each way betting market, particularly for outright winner selections. Major championships typically pay places at 1/4 or 1/5 odds for top five, top ten, or top twenty finishes depending on field size. Smaller tour events may pay fewer places, often just top five at 1/5 odds. The large competitive fields and unpredictable outcomes make each way betting attractive for golf, allowing stakes to be protected when selections finish near the lead without winning.

Rule 4 Deductions and How They Affect Each Way Bets

Rule 4 is a deduction mechanism applied when a horse is withdrawn from a race after betting has commenced. The deduction reduces winnings proportionally based on the withdrawn horse's odds to maintain fairness for bookmakers. Most basic each way calculators do not automatically apply Rule 4 adjustments to place terms or odds, requiring users to manually modify inputs or use advanced calculators with dedicated Rule 4 functionality.

When a non-runner is declared, the Rule 4 deduction percentage depends on the withdrawn horse's odds at the time of withdrawal. Shorter odds result in larger deductions. For example, a horse withdrawn at 2/1 triggers a 30p in the pound deduction, while a 10/1 withdrawal results in a 10p deduction. These deductions apply to both the win and place portions of an each way bet, reducing the overall return proportionally.

The each way calculator requires adjusted inputs to reflect Rule 4 accurately. Bettors must either reduce the odds manually before calculation or reduce the final returns by the deduction percentage. If a selection was placed at 8/1 and a 10p Rule 4 applies, the effective odds become approximately 6.2/1 for calculation purposes. The stake remains unchanged, but winnings decrease across both win and place components.

Adjusting Calculator Inputs for Rule 4

Advanced each way calculators may include a dedicated Rule 4 field where users can enter the deduction percentage directly. For basic calculators without this feature, the practical approach involves calculating returns at original odds, then applying the deduction percentage to the total. Alternatively, manually reducing the odds input before calculation provides an approximate result. When multiple non-runners occur, Rule 4 deductions compound, requiring cumulative adjustments to maintain accuracy in return projections.
