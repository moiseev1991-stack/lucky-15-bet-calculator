What Is a Lucky 63 Bet?

A Lucky 63 is a full-cover accumulator bet consisting of six selections that generate 63 separate bets. The structure includes 6 singles, 15 doubles, 20 trebles, 15 four-folds, 6 five-folds, and 1 six-fold accumulator. Each selection multiplies the stake by 63, meaning a £1 Lucky 63 costs £63 total stake. The return depends on how many selections win at their given odds.

The Lucky 63 covers every possible combination of the six selections, offering return potential even when not all picks win. Unlike a standard accumulator that requires all selections to win, a Lucky 63 pays out on any winning single bet. The profit increases as more selections win, with the six-fold accumulator delivering the maximum return when all six picks succeed.

Horse racing and football represent the most common sports for Lucky 63 bets in GB markets. Bettors select six horses, teams, or other outcomes across different races or matches. The calculator processes the odds for each selection and determines the total return across all 63 bet combinations.

Each way betting applies an additional layer to the Lucky 63 structure. When the each way option is selected, the bet doubles to 126 bets total (63 win bets plus 63 place bets). The place terms vary by sport and bookmaker, typically paying at 1/4 or 1/5 odds for finishing in the top positions. The stake requirement doubles accordingly, so a £1 each way Lucky 63 costs £126.

The profit calculation subtracts the total stake from the total return. If three selections win at odds of 3/1, 5/1, and 2/1, the calculator determines which of the 63 bets produce a return (the three singles, three doubles, and one treble in this scenario) and sums the individual returns. The remaining bets that include losing selections produce no return.

How Lucky 63 Bets Are Calculated

Singles, Doubles, and Trebles

Singles return the stake multiplied by the odds for each winning selection. For example, a £1 stake on a selection at 3/1 returns £4 (£3 profit plus the original stake). Doubles multiply the odds of two selections together: if two horses win at 3/1 and 2/1, a £1 double returns £12 (3 × 2 × £1 stake). Trebles work the same way with three selections. A treble combining winners at 3/1, 2/1, and 4/1 with a £1 stake returns £60 (3 × 2 × 4 × £1). The calculator processes all 41 of these smaller combinations automatically.

Four-Folds, Five-Folds, and Six-Fold Accumulator

The accumulator components multiply odds across four, five, or all six selections. A four-fold bet combining winners at 2/1, 3/1, 4/1, and 5/1 with a £1 stake returns £360 (2 × 3 × 4 × 5 × £1). Returns grow exponentially as more selections combine. The six-fold accumulator represents the highest-risk component: if all six selections win at average odds of 3/1, a £1 six-fold returns £729 (3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × £1). Each Way Lucky 63 Explained

Each way betting transforms a Lucky 63 into a 126-bet wager by placing two separate bets on each selection: one for the win and one for a place finish. This doubles the total stake, as every bet within the Lucky 63 structure is duplicated. Place bets typically pay at a fraction of the win odds, commonly one-quarter or one-fifth, depending on the event terms. This approach provides greater coverage but requires careful consideration of the increased cost.

How Each Way Betting Works

An each way bet consists of two equal stakes. The win portion pays out if the selection finishes first, while the place portion pays if the selection finishes within the specified place positions, usually determined by the number of runners and race type. In horse racing, place terms vary: races with five to seven runners may offer places for the top three at one-quarter odds, while larger fields might extend to four places at one-fifth odds.

Lucky 63 vs Heinz and Other Accumulator Bets

Lucky 63 vs Lucky 15

The Lucky 15 covers four selections across 15 bets: four singles, six doubles, four trebles, and one four-fold accumulator. This lower-cost option suits players with fewer confident picks. The Lucky 63 extends coverage to six selections, creating 63 bets with substantially higher stake requirements. Both include singles, providing a safety net if only one selection wins. Lucky 63 vs Heinz

Both the Lucky 63 and Heinz use six selections, but the Heinz excludes the six singles, reducing the total to 57 bets. The Heinz suits players confident that multiple selections will win, as the omission of singles lowers cost without sacrificing return on doubles and higher accumulators. The Lucky 63 provides a safety buffer through singles coverage, trading a modest stake increase for protection against isolated winners.
