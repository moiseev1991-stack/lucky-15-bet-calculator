What Is a Super Heinz Bet?

A Super Heinz is a multiple bet consisting of 7 selections that creates 120 separate bets. The bet includes 21 doubles, 35 trebles, 35 four-fold accumulators, 21 five-fold accumulators, 7 six-fold accumulators, and 1 seven-fold accumulator. At least 2 selections must win for any return, though larger returns require more winning selections. This betting structure appeals to bettors who want coverage across multiple outcomes without requiring all selections to win.

The super heinz calculator football tool originated in the United Kingdom betting markets. Football matches represent the most common application, where bettors combine multiple match outcomes across different fixtures. Horse Racing events also feature prominently, particularly during festival periods when multiple races take place on the same day. Boxing matches occasionally appear in Super Heinz combinations, especially during major fight cards.

The 120-bet structure provides extensive coverage but requires careful stake management. Each individual bet carries the same stake amount, meaning a £1 Super Heinz costs £120 in total. The super heinz calculator determines potential returns by calculating each of the 120 combinations separately, then summing the winning bets based on actual results.

Super Heinz Bet Structure - The 120 Combinations

A Super Heinz bet combines seven selections into 120 separate wagers across six different accumulator types. This full-coverage system originates from the United Kingdom betting tradition and requires all selections to be made before any events begin. The 120 bets consist of 21 doubles, 35 trebles, 35 four-folds, 21 five-folds, 7 six-folds, and 1 seven-fold accumulator. Each combination covers a different subset of the original seven selections.

How the Combinations Form

The mathematical structure behind a super heinz calculator divides seven selections (Team A through Team G) into every possible combination format. The 21 doubles represent every two-selection pairing: A+B, A+C, A+D, and so on through F+G. The 35 trebles cover every three-selection group: A+B+C, A+B+D, continuing until E+F+G. This pattern continues through four-selection, five-selection, and six-selection groupings.

For four-folds, 35 bets combine selections like A+B+C+D, A+B+C+E, through D+E+F+G. The 21 five-folds require five winning selections from groups such as A+B+C+D+E. Seven six-folds each omit one selection: all except Team A, all except Team B, and so forth. The single seven-fold accumulator requires all selections to win.

Minimum Winners Required

At least two selections must win for any return, as the doubles represent the smallest accumulator type included. A single winner produces no payout because no bet type covers individual selections. Three winners activate one double (any pair from the three) plus one treble (all three together). This partial coverage characteristic distinguishes the Super Heinz from single bets or simpler accumulators, offering returns even when several selections fail while carrying the risk of total loss if fewer than two succeed.
