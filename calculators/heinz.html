What is a Heinz Bet?

A Heinz bet is a multiple bet consisting of 6 selections that creates 57 individual bets. The name derives from the Heinz 57 varieties slogan. This bet type combines 15 doubles, 20 trebles, 15 four-fold accumulators, 6 five-fold accumulators, and 1 six-fold accumulator from the chosen selections.

The structure of a Heinz bet means that only two selections need to win for a return, though the profit depends on how many selections succeed and their odds. Each of the 57 bets is treated as a separate wager, so the total stake equals the unit stake multiplied by 57.

Heinz bets are commonly placed on football matches, particularly Premier League fixtures, and horse racing events where bettors identify six selections they expect to win. The bet type appeals to those seeking coverage across multiple outcomes without requiring all selections to win for a return.

The breakdown works as follows: the 15 doubles pair each selection with every other selection, the 20 trebles create all possible three-selection combinations, the 15 four-fold accumulators combine four selections, the 6 five-fold accumulators use five selections, and the single six-fold accumulator includes all selections. This structure provides returns even when only two selections win, making it less risky than a standard accumulator requiring all selections to succeed.

Understanding the composition of the 57 bets helps bettors assess the total stake required and potential returns. A calculator simplifies this process by showing exactly how the stake is distributed across doubles, trebles, and accumulators, along with potential winnings for different winning scenarios.

The 57 Bet Combinations Explained

The heinz calculator generates 57 bets from 6 selections through combinatorial mathematics. These comprise 15 doubles (pairing any two selections), 20 trebles (combining any three), 15 four-fold accumulators (grouping any four), 6 five-fold accumulators (combining any five), and 1 six-fold accumulator (all selections together). The structure derives from the binomial coefficient formula, which calculates how many ways selections can be combined at each level.

When selections are labeled A through F, the doubles include combinations like A+B, A+C, through to E+F. Trebles expand to combinations such as A+B+C, A+B+D, continuing through D+E+F. The pattern continues upward through increasingly larger accumulator groupings. Each combination represents an independent bet, meaning at least one selection must win for any return, though multiple winners are typically needed to generate profit given the total stake required.

Each-Way Heinz Bets

An each-way Heinz bet doubles the total number of bets from 57 to 114, with 57 bets placed on win outcomes and 57 on place outcomes. This structure provides coverage across both winning and placing selections, commonly used in horse racing events like the Grand National and Cheltenham Festival where place terms offer value.

Place terms typically pay at one-quarter or one-fifth of the original odds for positions 2nd through 4th in competitive races. If a selection has odds of 5/1 and the place terms are one-quarter, the place portion pays at 5/4 odds. This calculation means that even when win bets fail, place returns provide a safety net that can reduce overall losses or generate modest profits.
