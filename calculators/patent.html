What Is a Patent Bet?

A Patent is a multiple bet that combines 3 selections into 7 separate bets: 3 singles, 3 doubles, and 1 treble. This structure provides a safety net for bettors, requiring at least one winning selection to generate a return. Unlike accumulator bets where all selections must win, the Patent offers partial returns when only one or two selections succeed, making it a popular choice for managing risk across multiple outcomes.

The 7 bets within a Patent are structured as follows: each of the 3 selections creates an individual Single bet, each pair of selections forms a Double, and all 3 selections together create one Treble. This combination means bettors can profit even when not all selections win. If all 3 selections are successful, all 7 bets pay out, maximizing returns.

Patent bets are widely used in horse racing and football betting, particularly when bettors identify 3 strong selections but want protection against one or two losing. The format is considered beginner-friendly among multiple bets because it balances potential returns with reduced risk compared to trebles or accumulators.

Each Way Patents are also common, doubling the number of bets to 14 by placing Each Way wagers on each selection. This adds place returns to the calculation, particularly valuable in horse racing where Each Way terms apply. The calculator accounts for Each Way places and adjusts payouts accordingly.

Rule 4 deductions may apply to Patent bets when non-runners are withdrawn from horse races. The calculator incorporates Rule 4 adjustments to provide accurate return estimates after deductions. Bettors enter the Rule 4 percentage for any affected selections, and the calculator recalculates odds across all 7 bets to reflect the reduced payout.

The Patent structure differs from other multiple bets like the Trixie, which also uses 3 selections but excludes the 3 singles, creating only 4 bets total. The inclusion of singles in a Patent ensures returns are possible with just one winner, whereas a Trixie requires at least two winning selections.

Understanding the 7-Bet Structure

A patent bet calculator processes seven distinct wagers derived from three selections. This structure ensures coverage across multiple outcome scenarios while maintaining a controlled stake exposure. The 7-bet composition comprises three single bets, three double bets, and one treble bet, each calculated independently before aggregation into Total Return and Total Profit metrics.

When three selections are designated as A, B, and C, the single bets cover each selection individually. Selection A stands alone as one bet, selection B as a second, and selection C as a third. These single bets return a profit if their respective selection wins, regardless of other outcomes.

The three double bets combine pairs: AB links the first and second selections, AC pairs the first and third, and BC joins the second and third. Each double requires both constituent selections to win for a return. The odds multiply together, then apply to the stake allocated to that specific double.

The treble bet ABC demands all three selections to succeed. This combination offers the highest potential return within the patent structure, as the odds of all three selections multiply sequentially. The patent calculator computes this by chaining the decimal or fractional odds across A, B, and C, then applying the unit stake.

Each Way Patents Explained

Each Way Patents combine win and place betting across three selections, doubling the number of individual bets from seven to fourteen. Players place stakes on three Singles, three Doubles, and one Treble for both win and place outcomes, creating a total of fourteen separate wagers. This structure provides coverage when selections finish placed rather than winning outright, making it particularly valuable in horse racing where place positions offer returns at reduced odds.

The patent calculator processes both win and place stakes separately, calculating Total Return and Total Profit based on outcomes across all fourteen bets. Each Way betting requires understanding how place terms affect final payouts, as odds fractions determine placed-horse returns. Stake requirements double compared to standard Patents, but the additional coverage protects against near-miss scenarios where selections finish in paying positions without winning.

Place Terms and Payout Calculation

Place terms specify the fraction of win odds paid for placed finishes, typically one-quarter odds for races with five to seven runners offering two or three places. A horse priced at 5/1 returns 1.25/1 for a place finish when one-quarter terms apply, calculated by dividing the win odds by four. The patent calculator applies these fractions to each Single, Double, and Treble placed component.

Different race conditions affect place terms, with larger fields sometimes offering one-fifth odds across four places. Rule 4 deductions apply to both win and place elements when non-runners reduce field size, proportionally lowering returns across affected bets. Players must verify place terms before confirming stakes, as variations significantly impact Total Return calculations when selections place without winning outright.

When to Use a Patent Bet

The patent calculator proves most effective when backing three selections with moderate confidence rather than certainty. Unlike a straight accumulator requiring all legs to win, this structure tolerates one failure while still returning a profit if the other two selections succeed at reasonable odds.

Horse racing attracts Patent use frequently, particularly when targeting three horses across different meetings. Each race operates independently, reducing correlation risk. Football markets suit Patents when combining matches from various leagues or betting on outcomes with similar probability profiles.

The stake commitment deserves consideration. A £1 unit Patent costs £7, while each-way versions double this to £14. Players comparing a Patent against standalone singles should weigh the protection value: if confidence feels uncertain, the patent calculator football structure mitigates risk without sacrificing upside when all win.

Patents work less effectively with heavy favourites, as short odds rarely cover the seven-bet outlay unless all three win. Conversely, three long-shots may offer impressive treble returns but leave the Patent unprofitable if only one or two succeed. The sweet spot lies between evens and 5/1, where doubles provide meaningful insurance. Premier League betting often falls within this range, making Patents practical for weekend accumulators.
