What is a Canadian Bet?

A Canadian bet is a full-cover multiple wager consisting of 26 separate bets derived from 5 selections. Also known as a Super Yankee, this betting structure combines every possible combination of doubles, trebles, four-folds, and one five-fold accumulator from the chosen selections. The Canadian bet requires at least two selections to win for any returns, making it less risky than a standard accumulator while offering substantial profit potential when multiple selections succeed.

The 26-bet composition breaks down into specific groupings: 10 doubles covering every two-selection pairing, 10 trebles covering every three-selection combination, 5 four-folds covering every four-selection combination, and 1 five-fold accumulator combining all selections. Breaking Down the 26 Bets

The mathematical structure of a Canadian bet derives from combinatorial principles applied to 5 selections. The 10 doubles emerge from pairing any two selections from five options (calculated as C(5,2) in mathematical notation). The 10 trebles represent every possible three-selection combination (C(5,3)), while the 5 four-folds cover each four-selection grouping (C(5,4)). Finally, the single five-fold accumulator combines all selections into one bet.

This totals precisely 26 individual wagers: 10 + 10 + 5 + 1 = 26. Each bet within the Canadian structure operates independently, meaning partial success yields returns even when not all selections win. The stake multiplies by 26 to cover each component bet, so a £1 Canadian bet costs £26 total. How to Calculate Canadian Bet Returns

Calculating Canadian bet returns requires determining which combinations win based on successful selections, then summing the individual returns from each winning bet. The total stake (base stake multiplied by 26) is subtracted from total returns to establish profit. Canadian Bet Calculator automates this process by evaluating all 26 component bets against the outcomes and applying the decimal odds multiplication for each winning combination.

The calculation complexity increases with partial wins. When all 5 selections win, every bet within the Super Yankee succeeds—all doubles, trebles, four-folds, and the five-fold accumulator pay out. With 4 winners, the five-fold fails but multiple doubles, trebles, and four-folds succeed. With 3 winners, only doubles and trebles return stakes. With 2 winners, only the relevant doubles generate returns. One or zero winners results in total stake loss.

Calculating Individual Bet Returns

Each individual bet within the Canadian structure calculates returns by multiplying the stake per bet by the decimal odds of each selection in that combination. For a double with selections at 2.0 and 3.0 decimal odds, a £1 stake returns £6 (2.0 × 3.0 × £1). A treble multiplies three odds values together, a four-fold multiplies four, and the five-fold multiplies all five.

The stake per bet equals the total stake divided by 26. A £26 total stake allocates £1 to each of the 26 component bets. Canadian Bet Calculator performs these multiplications across all winning combinations simultaneously, then aggregates the returns. The decimal odds format simplifies calculation compared to fractional odds, as multiplication directly produces the total return including the original stake.

Minimum Winning Selections for Returns

A Canadian bet requires at least 2 selections to win for any return, because the doubles represent the smallest bet component within the 26-bet structure. If only one selection wins, all doubles, trebles, four-folds, and the five-fold accumulator fail, resulting in complete stake loss. This two-winner minimum provides significantly better odds of partial returns compared to a standard five-fold accumulator, which demands all 5 selections to succeed.

The risk profile shifts based on selection confidence. Conservative bettors appreciate the cushion of potential returns from just two winners, while aggressive bettors targeting maximum profit need all five selections to land. Each-Way Canadian Bets

An each-way Canadian bet doubles the total number of wagers to 52 bets, combining 26 win bets with 26 place bets across the same 5 selections. This structure proves particularly valuable in horse racing and greyhound racing where place terms provide returns even when selections finish outside the winning position, typically paying at one-quarter or one-fifth of the win odds for the top three or four finishers depending on the field size.

The doubled stake requirement means a £1 each-way Canadian costs £52 total rather than £26 for win-only bets. This increased outlay buys additional insurance through place coverage, which can deliver returns when several selections finish strongly without winning. Place terms vary by event: larger fields in major horse racing festivals often pay four places, while smaller races may only pay three.

Common Canadian Bet Mistakes to Avoid

Understanding the true cost and structural requirements of Canadian bets prevents expensive errors. The most frequent mistake involves underestimating the total stake: a £1 Canadian bet actually costs £26, representing 26 individual bets combined. Many bettors confuse this with a simple £1 accumulator, discovering the actual commitment only at placement. Equally problematic are correlated selections that reduce the bet's independence and value.

Common pitfalls include selecting correlated outcomes within the same match, such as a team to win combined with over 2.5 goals in that fixture. These selections lack true independence, reducing the Canadian bet's diversification benefit. The Super Yankee structure, comprising 26 bets across 5 selections including 10 doubles, 10 trebles, five four-folds, and one five-fold, requires genuine independence between picks.
