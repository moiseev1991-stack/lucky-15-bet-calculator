What Is a Goliath Bet?

A Goliath bet consists of 247 separate bets derived from 8 selections. The structure comprises 28 doubles, 56 trebles, 70 four-folds, 56 five-folds, 28 six-folds, 8 seven-folds, and 1 eight-fold accumulator. This multiple bet type takes its name from the biblical giant, reflecting the massive number of combinations generated from relatively few selections.

The Goliath bet belongs to the full-cover family of betting systems. Unlike a straight accumulator requiring all selections to win, the Goliath structure allows returns even when some selections lose. Each combination within the 247 bets operates independently, meaning partial success generates payouts from the winning doubles, trebles, or other multiples that remain intact.

How Goliath Bets Work: Understanding the 247 Combinations

The 247 combinations arise from mathematical principles of selection grouping. From 8 selections, the calculator generates every possible pairing (28 doubles), every possible trio (56 trebles), and continues through all larger groupings up to the single eight-fold accumulator. The formula for combinations determines the exact count: 8 choose 2 yields 28 doubles, 8 choose 3 produces 56 trebles, following the pattern through all bet types.

Each of the 247 bets operates as a separate wager with its own stake. When selections win, every combination containing those winning selections pays out at the combined odds of the selections within that specific bet. A scenario with 5 winning selections triggers payouts from all doubles containing any 2 of those 5 winners, all trebles containing any 3 of the 5 winners, and all four-folds and five-folds that can be formed from the winning selections.

The structure provides built-in insurance against complete loss. Even with only 2 selections winning from the original 8, the bettor receives returns from the single double formed by those 2 winners. Three winners generate returns from 3 doubles and 1 treble. The payout scale increases exponentially as more selections succeed, with 7 winners activating 21 doubles, 35 trebles, 35 four-folds, 21 five-folds, 7 six-folds, and 1 seven-fold.

Total Stake Requirements: Why Unit Stake Multiplies by 247

The total stake equals the unit stake multiplied by 247 bets. A 10p unit stake requires £24.70 total outlay (10p × 247). A £1 unit stake costs £247. This multiplication applies universally across all Goliath bets regardless of the odds or selections chosen.

Bettors cannot place partial Goliath bets. The system requires all 247 combinations as a mandatory package. The unit stake determines the cost per individual bet within the structure, while the total stake represents the complete investment across all combinations. This stake multiplication distinguishes Goliath bets from single accumulators, where one stake covers one bet. The Goliath calculator computes returns by applying the unit stake to each winning combination separately, then summing all successful bet payouts to determine the total return.

  2. Calculate all winning doubles by multiplying odds for every pair of winning selections
  3. Calculate all winning trebles by multiplying odds for every trio of winning selections

  5. Calculate the eight-fold accumulator if all eight selections win

Common Goliath Bet Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Players frequently misunderstand the financial commitment required for Goliath bets, leading to unexpected losses. The most common errors involve miscalculating total stake, overestimating returns from partial wins, and failing to assess whether selected odds justify the 247-bet structure. A goliath calculator helps identify these pitfalls before placement.

  1. Stake Confusion — A £1 Goliath does not cost £1 total. The unit stake multiplies across all 247 bets, meaning a £1 Goliath requires £247. Players who expect to risk only their stated unit stake face an unpleasant surprise at checkout. Always calculate total stake (unit stake × 247) before confirming placement.
  2. Unrealistic Return Expectations — Two short-priced winners generate minimal return.   3. Ignoring Odds Requirements — Goliath bets require competitive odds across all eight selections to approach break-even. Favourites priced below evens (2.00) across the entire selection set rarely generate profit even with five or six winners.
