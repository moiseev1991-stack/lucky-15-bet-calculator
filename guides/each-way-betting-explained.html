What is Each Way Betting?

An each way bet consists of two simultaneous bets on the same selection: one bet on the selection to win and one bet on the selection to place. Players pay double the stated stake because they are placing two separate bets. The win bet pays out at full odds if the selection wins outright, while the place bet pays at a fraction of the win odds if the selection finishes within the designated paying positions.

The fundamental difference between an each way bet and a straight win bet lies in the dual structure. A £10 each way bet costs £20 total: £10 on the win bet and £10 on the place bet. If the selection wins, both components pay out. If the selection places but does not win, only the place bet pays out, with the win bet lost.

Each way betting originated in horse racing but has expanded to other sports. In football, bookmakers offer each way markets on competitions like top goalscorer or tournament winner. The number of places that qualify for the place bet varies by event and bookmaker. A typical each way bet example might offer four places in a major tournament, while smaller competitions may offer only two or three places.

Understanding how many places each way a bookmaker pays is critical before placing the bet. Major horse racing events like the Grand National typically pay more places than standard races. In football markets, the Open Championship or World Cup top scorer markets often feature extended place terms compared to domestic league top scorer bets.

The place component provides insurance against near-misses. If a selection finishes second in a race with three paying places, the place bet returns a profit even though the win bet loses. This risk reduction comes at the cost of doubled stakes, making each way betting a strategic choice rather than a default option.

Both accumulator bets and BTTS markets can incorporate each way betting principles in certain contexts, though traditional each way structure applies primarily to single selections in outright markets.

How Each Way Betting Works: Win and Place Components

Each way betting splits a single wager into two distinct components that operate under different payout rules. The win component pays at the full advertised odds only if the selection finishes first. The place component pays at a fraction of the win odds if the selection finishes within the specified paying positions, which includes first place. When a selection wins, both the win bet and place bet pay out simultaneously.

The win bet operates identically to a standard win-only bet. A £10 win bet at 8/1 odds returns £90 total if successful: £80 profit plus the £10 stake. The place bet pays at reduced odds calculated by applying the place fraction to the win odds. If the same 8/1 selection places but does not win, the place bet pays at the reduced rate determined by the bookmaker's place terms, typically one-quarter or one-fifth of the win odds.

Both components share the same base odds, but the place fraction determines the actual payout for the place bet. A selection that finishes in a paying place position triggers the place payout regardless of whether it wins. This means a second-place finish still generates a return from the place component, covering part of the total stake even though the win bet loses.

The dual structure creates three possible outcomes. If the selection wins, both bets pay. If the selection places without winning, only the place bet pays. If the selection finishes outside the paying positions, both bets lose. The place terms define how many positions qualify for the place payout, typically ranging from two to four places depending on field size and event prestige.

In football markets, each way betting applies to outright markets like tournament winners or top goalscorer competitions. The Grand National and Open Championship traditionally offer extended place terms due to large fields. Accumulator builders sometimes incorporate each way selections when targeting long-odds outcomes with place insurance, though standard accumulators and BTTS bets operate on different principles.

Place Odds Fractions: 1/4 vs 1/5 Explained

Place odds fractions determine the payout rate for the place component of each way bets. The two most common fractions are 1/4 and 1/5 of the win odds. These fractions multiply against the original odds to calculate the place payout odds.

A selection at 10/1 with 1/4 place terms pays 2.5/1 for a place finish. The calculation divides the 10/1 odds by four, resulting in 2.5/1. A £10 place bet at 2.5/1 returns £35 total: £25 profit plus the £10 stake. The same 10/1 selection with 1/5 place terms pays 2/1 for a place, returning £30 total on a £10 stake.

The difference between 1/4 and 1/5 terms significantly impacts returns. Bookmakers typically offer 1/5 odds in larger fields with more paying places, while smaller fields receive 1/4 odds with fewer paying positions. Horse racing events with 16 or more runners often feature 1/4 odds for three or four places, whereas major handicaps may offer 1/5 odds for five or six places. Understanding which fraction applies before betting determines the potential place return.

Horse Racing Each Way Bet Example

A £10 each way bet on a horse at 10/1 odds in the Grand National with standard 1/4 odds for four places costs £20 total—£10 on the win and £10 on the place. If the horse wins, the win portion returns £110 (£100 profit plus £10 stake) and the place portion returns £35 (£25 profit at 10/4 odds plus £10 stake), producing total returns of £145 and net profit of £125. If the horse finishes second, third or fourth, only the place bet pays, returning £35 for a net loss of £5 on the £20 total stake. If the horse finishes fifth or lower, both bets lose and the entire £20 stake is lost. This structure means a placed horse nearly recovers the stake, whilst a winner delivers substantial profit.
