Patent Bet Definition: 7 Bets from 3 Selections

A Patent Bet is a full-cover betting system that transforms three individual selections into seven separate bets. The structure includes three singles, three doubles, and one treble, creating a safety net that delivers returns even if only one selection wins. This format differs from a standard accumulator, where all selections must succeed for any payout. The Patent covers every possible winning combination from the chosen trio.

The seven-bet breakdown works as follows: each of the three selections generates its own single bet, ensuring a return if that choice wins at odds above evens. The three doubles pair each selection with the other two, while the single treble combines all three. This construction means a bettor stakes seven units total—one unit per bet. A £1 Patent Bet costs £7, as each component receives an equal stake.

Horse racing traditionally hosts the majority of Patent Bets, though football markets now frequently feature this structure. The format serves bettors who back three selections but want downside protection. Unlike a Trixie Bet, which omits the singles and contains only four bets (three doubles and one treble), the Patent includes those crucial individual wagers. This distinction matters when confidence varies across selections.

The Patent also contrasts with a Yankee Bet, which expands the concept to four selections and eleven bets (six doubles, four trebles, one fourfold). The Yankee omits singles entirely, similar to the Trixie. The Patent's inclusion of singles makes it more forgiving for scenarios where only one or two selections win, while still offering accumulator-style returns when all three succeed. This balance between risk and reward defines the Patent's appeal in 2026.

How a Patent Bet Works: Structure and Minimum Winners

Understanding the Patent Bet requires clarity on how three selections—labelled A, B, and C—produce seven bets. The singles component covers Selection A alone, Selection B alone, and Selection C alone. These three bets activate individually, meaning a winning selection at favourable odds generates a return regardless of the other two outcomes. If Horse A wins at 3/1 but Horses B and C lose, the bettor still collects on the single for A.

The doubles layer pairs the selections: one bet combines A and B, another combines A and C, and the third joins B and C. Each double requires both selections within it to win for a payout. If Horses A and B both win, the AB double pays out, alongside the singles for A and B. The doubles multiply the odds of their two components, creating higher returns than singles alone.

The treble forms the final bet, combining all three selections into one accumulator. All three must win for this bet to pay. When Horse A, Horse B, and Horse C all succeed, the treble multiplies the odds of all three, delivering the largest return within the Patent structure. Alongside this treble payout, the bettor also collects on all three singles and all three doubles, maximising total winnings.

Minimum winner scenarios clarify the Patent's value. With one winner, only that selection's single pays. If Horse A wins at 2/1 with a £1 stake, the return is £3 (£2 profit plus £1 stake), against a £7 total outlay. Two winners trigger two singles plus one double. If Horses A and B win, the bettor collects on singles A and B, plus the AB double. Three winners activate all seven bets, paying three singles, three doubles, and one treble. This structure mirrors patterns found in football betting, where Team A, Team B, and Team C can replace the horse racing selections, applying the same seven-bet logic.

Calculating Patent Bet Returns: Worked Examples

Understanding potential returns helps bettors assess whether a Patent Bet offers value across different winning scenarios. The structure includes three Singles, three Doubles, and one Treble, creating seven separate bets that generate returns when one, two, or all three selections succeed. At £1 per bet, the total stake reaches £7, making it essential to calculate exact returns for each outcome.

Returns When One Selection Wins

When only one selection wins, just a single bet pays out from the seven-bet structure. Using Horse A at 3/1 odds with a £1 stake, the return equals £4 (£3 profit plus £1 stake returned). With a £7 total stake, this scenario produces a £3 loss overall. For bettors to break even when only one selection wins, that horse needs odds above 6/1. Shorter odds require multiple winners to generate profit, which demonstrates why careful selection matters in Patent Bet construction across Horse Racing or Football markets.

Returns When Two Selections Win

Two winning selections activate three bets: two Singles and one Double. If Horse A (3/1) and Horse B (5/2) both win, the Singles return £4 and £4.50 respectively. The Double combines both odds: (3/1 + 1) × (5/2 + 1) × £1 stake = 4 × 3.5 = £14. Total return reaches £22.50 from these three bets, delivering £15.50 profit against the £7 stake. This scenario shows how Doubles multiply odds together rather than simply adding them, creating significantly better returns than Singles alone.

Maximum Returns: All Three Selections Win

When all three selections succeed, every bet component pays out. Adding Horse C at 2/1 to the previous winners generates returns from three Singles (£4 + £4.50 + £3), three Doubles (A+B £14, A+C £12, B+C £10.50), and one Treble. The Treble multiplies all three odds: 4 × 3.5 × 3 × £1 = £42. Total return: £90 from the £7 stake, producing £83 profit. Compared to a standard Treble accumulator, a Patent Bet provides identical Treble returns but adds safety through Singles and Doubles, answering whether is patent bet worth it for those seeking balanced risk across Yankee Bet or Trixie Bet alternatives.

Each Way Patent Bets: Doubling Your Stake for Place Coverage

An each way Patent Bet doubles the bet count to 14, covering both win and place outcomes for all seven bet components. This structure costs £14 at £1 per bet (£7 win, £7 place), providing returns even when selections finish in placed positions rather than winning outright. Each way terms in Horse Racing typically pay 1/4 or 1/5 of the win odds for the top two, three, or four finishers depending on field size.

The 14-bet structure breaks down as follows:

  1. Three win Singles plus three place Singles
  2. Three win Doubles plus three place Doubles
  3. One win Treble plus one place Treble

When a horse finishes second at 4/1 odds with 1/4 place terms, the place portion returns £2 (4/1 ÷ 4 = 1/1, paying £2 including stake). This place coverage proves valuable when one or two selections narrowly miss winning but still finish in paying positions. Combined with Best Odds Guaranteed at UK bookmakers, each way patents on Horse Racing provide protection against late odds movements while maintaining place insurance.

The doubled stake requires careful consideration in Football markets where each way betting typically applies only to specific tournaments with multiple place-paying positions. Compared to standard Trixie Bet or Yankee Bet structures, the Patent Bet offers Singles protection, making the each way option particularly suitable when confidence varies across the three selections rather than backing all equally for outright victories.

Common Patent Betting Mistakes to Avoid

Understanding what is a patent bet requires recognising where punters commonly go wrong. One frequent error involves selecting extremely short odds across all three selections. Unless each selection exceeds evens (2.00), single-win returns rarely cover the £7 stake, making the bet unprofitable even with one winner.

Many bettors also ignore correlation between selections. Choosing three favourites from the same race creates a scenario where if one loses, the others likely fail too—linked outcomes reduce patent bets' protective value.

Misunderstanding each way implications causes costly mistakes. Players forget that each way patents require 14 bets (£14 minimum stake for £1 units), then discover place terms don't justify the doubled outlay. Checking place odds and number of places paid before committing prevents disappointment.

Another common pitfall involves using patents when confidence levels suggest simpler bet types. If genuinely certain about all three selections winning, a straightforward treble offers better returns without covering partial outcomes that may never materialise.

When asking is patent bet worth it, consider these mistakes. Patent bets suit scenarios with genuine uncertainty across multiple events, not as blanket solutions for every accumulator situation.
